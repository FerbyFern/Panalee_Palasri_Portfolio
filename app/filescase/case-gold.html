02 — Problem Statement

A Volatile Business Without a System to Match

Gold trading is fast, high-value, and high-risk. Every second of price delay or data inconsistency is a business liability.

Core Problem
"Chiab Seng Heng's gold trading operations span multiple departments, 9 user roles, and thousands of daily transactions — all managed without a unified, real-time system capable of locking deal prices, tracking net positions, and providing a full audit trail."
  • Gold prices change every few seconds. Without a system to instantly lock the agreed price at Deal creation, discrepancies between what was agreed and what was recorded create financial risk and disputes.
  • Transactions flow across Sales → Accounting → Finance → Refinery departments. Without a shared platform, each department works from fragmented information — causing delays, errors, and duplicated effort.
  • Net Open Position (NOP) — the critical measure of the business's gold exposure — cannot be tracked in real time, leaving management unable to respond to price movements or make informed hedging decisions.
  • Container-based inventory (tracking gold by physical tray, box, or pin using LPN codes) is error-prone without a system, making stock discrepancies difficult to detect or trace.
  • Old gold buyback requires solder (น้ำประสาน) deduction calculations that are currently performed manually, leading to inconsistent results and potential disputes with customers.
  • Nine distinct user roles with different permissions require a strict access control system — which cannot be reliably enforced through manual processes or simple tools.
  • There is no structured audit trail. Without one, the business cannot demonstrate compliance, resolve disputes, or trace the source of errors back to a specific user and timestamp.
⚡
Price Volatility Risk
Gold prices change in seconds. Every transaction must be timestamped and price-locked at the moment of agreement — not when paperwork catches up.
🔗
Fragmented Workflow
5 departments (Sales, Accounting, Finance, Refinery, Trader) with no shared data layer — creating handoff delays, lost records, and parallel reconciliation work.
📊
Invisible Risk Exposure
Without real-time NOP tracking, management cannot see whether the business is Long or Short on gold at any moment — a critical gap in risk management.
🔒
No Audit Trail
No structured log of who did what, when, and what changed. Any dispute, error, or compliance question is nearly impossible to resolve with confidence.
⚖️
Calculation Inconsistency
Manual solder deduction, labor cost calculation, and credit limit checks are inconsistent across staff — creating customer experience problems and margin leakage.
🏭
Refinery Tracking Gap
Gold sent to refinery for processing enters a black box — no WIP status, no weight tracking, no automatic reconciliation when returned. High risk of loss or dispute.
03 — Research & Discovery

Building Understanding First

Designing a gold trading system requires deep domain knowledge before a single wireframe is drawn. We invested heavily in understanding the business before designing the solution.

🎯
Research Goals
Understand the gold trading workflow end-to-end — from price setting and receiving to settlement, NOP calculation, and refinery operations. Map each of 9 roles to their specific tasks, data needs, and decision points.
🗣️
Stakeholder Interviews
Conducted interviews with all key roles: Business Owner (Chiab Seng Heng), Process Improvement Advisor (Dr. Vatcharapol), Project Manager, Lead Developer, and system users across Sales, Accounting, Finance, and Refinery departments.
📜
Domain Research
Deep research into Thai gold trading terminology and industry standards: Ticket/Deal mechanics, FIFO allocation, NOP calculation, Thai Gold Traders Association weight standards (1 บาท = 15.2440g for gold bar, 15.1600g for jewelry), solder deduction rules.
🔧
Hardware Integration Research
Investigated scanner and weighing machine integration — a key business requirement. The system must accept Container ID from barcode/QR scanners and weight values from connected scales, bypassing manual data entry for speed and accuracy.
🗺️
Business Process Mapping
Mapped all 11 core business workflows as process flow diagrams (recorded in FSD). Each flow was reviewed with stakeholders to verify accuracy before UI design began, establishing a shared understanding of every system state and transition.
🔐
Access & Security Analysis
Analysed permission requirements for all 9 roles across 11 modules. Produced a full RBAC matrix showing C/E/D/V permissions and additional privileges (e.g., Approve Settlement, Manual Price Override, Intercompany Transfer) to guide UI access control design.
Critical Finding — Price Lock
"Once a Deal or Ticket is confirmed, the price must be locked and immutable. Any subsequent change to the gold market price cannot retroactively affect a confirmed transaction — this is both a business requirement and a regulatory necessity."
Critical Finding — FIFO Complexity
"Net Settlement must process account ledger entries (ป้าย) in FIFO order, automatically moving fully-settled entries to 'Empty' status. But users need the flexibility to override FIFO allocation for operational reasons — a tension that shapes the entire Settlement UI."
Critical Finding — Role Speed Difference
"Sales staff need to create transactions in seconds — they're talking to a customer. Accounting and Finance staff need the same data, but with more time to verify, attach evidence, and post to the ledger. The same data must support two very different workflows and time pressures."
Critical Finding — Old Gold Complexity
"Old gold buyback is not a simple transaction. It requires identifying the type of old gold, applying solder deduction rules (น้ำประสาน), assigning a 'Old Gold' status in the system automatically, and displaying remaining old gold inventory broken down by sub-type. This is entirely invisible to standard e-commerce inventory thinking."
04 — Define & Ideate

Translating Domain Knowledge Into Design Direction

With the business process fully mapped, we turned insights into design principles, HMW questions, and a role-permission framework that guided every screen decision.

How Might We Questions

Speed & Accuracy
How might we enable a Sales staff member to create a Deal and lock a gold price in under 30 seconds — without sacrificing the audit information the system needs to record?
Role Clarity
How might we design one system that presents each of 9 user roles with exactly the information and actions relevant to them — without building 9 separate interfaces?
NOP Visibility
How might we surface Net Open Position (NOP) in a way that a Trader or Owner can read the business's gold exposure at a glance and act within seconds of a price movement?
SKU Complexity
How might we expose an 8-level SKU hierarchy to users without requiring them to understand the full structure — letting them search, scan, or navigate to what they need quickly?
Settlement Flow
How might we make Net Settlement and FIFO allocation intuitive for Accounting staff who are balancing multiple open Tickets against available transactions simultaneously?
Audit & Compliance
How might we make the audit trail feel invisible to users doing their work, while ensuring every action — create, edit, approve, cancel, override — is captured with timestamp and user context?

Design Principles

01
Speed Above All for Transactional Roles
Sales and Trading interfaces must be optimised for speed — keyboard-first, minimal clicks, scanner-compatible — because every second costs money in a volatile gold market.
02
Data Density Without Overwhelm
Enterprise users need to see a lot of data simultaneously. The design must be information-rich without being visually chaotic — clear hierarchy, consistent type scale, and decisive use of colour to encode status.
03
Role-Adaptive, Not Role-Separate
One design system adapts to 9 roles by showing or hiding modules, actions, and data fields based on the logged-in role. The interface learns who you are and adjusts — it doesn't force users into separate products.
04
Immutability as a Visual Signal
Once a Deal is confirmed, it is locked. The UI must visually communicate this state — grayed edit controls, lock icons, read-only fields — so users never try to change something that cannot be changed.
05
Hardware-First Data Entry
Barcode scanner and weighing machine inputs must feel native — scan a Container ID or place gold on a scale, and the system receives the data. Manual typing is the fallback, not the primary mode.
06
Auditability Is Infrastructure
Every action writes to the audit log. Users don't need to think about this — but the system must expose a clear, queryable history view for Admins and Owners at any time.

SKU Hierarchy Navigation
Designed an 8-level SKU navigation component — users can drill down from Asset Division → Product Category → Sub-Category → Design → Supplier → Weight → Size, or scan a product barcode to jump directly to the matching SKU. Partial-match search allows typing any segment of the SKU string to narrow results quickly.
Net Settlement & Allocation Interface
The Settlement screen shows open Tickets on one side and available transactions (ป้าย) on the other — ordered by date for FIFO guidance but allowing free selection. Running totals update in real time as users allocate items, with a clear "Remaining" counter that must reach zero to complete settlement.
Container/LPN Inventory View
Inventory is viewed by Chamber → Layer → Container. Each Container shows its LPN code, type (Pin/Tray/Box/Bag), current contents by SKU, and weight. Scanning a Container barcode jumps directly to that Container's contents page. Movement history is accessible from any Container record.
Financial Ledger & Payment Attachment
Finance staff see a transaction queue with payment status (Pending / Paid / Partial). Each transaction supports payment method selection, amount entry, and file attachment (slip/evidence upload). VAT and WHT toggle independently per transaction, with calculated amounts shown inline before confirmation.

08 — Reflection & Learnings

What This Project Has Taught Me

Designing an enterprise system for a specialised industry requires a very different mindset from consumer product design. Here is what I have taken away from this project so far.

✅ What's Going Well

  • 📜The FSD-first approach — documenting every business rule and acceptance criterion before designing screens — has prevented scope creep and kept the design grounded in real business needs, not designer assumptions.
  • 🏦Deep domain immersion paid off. Understanding Thai gold trading terminology (NOP, ป้าย, มาทอง, ไปเงิน, รวดรัด) before designing allowed the UI to speak the language of its users, not translate it.
  • 🔐Designing the RBAC matrix before any screen was particularly valuable — it forced early decisions about what each role should see or not see, preventing costly redesigns during development.
  • 🤝Stakeholder sign-off on FSD v1.1 — across the PM, Lead Developer, Principal Software Engineer, Process Advisor, and Business Owner — created a shared contract that protects the design vision through development.
  • ⚡Thinking about hardware integration (scanner, scale) from day one shaped the entire transaction entry UX to be fast and error-resistant, not just visually polished.

⚠️ Challenges & What I'd Do Differently

  • 🧪Designing without UAT creates uncertainty. I've made assumptions about how Sales staff will use the Ticket creation screen under time pressure — these assumptions need real-world validation before we can be confident the flow is fast enough.
  • 📱The current design is desktop-first. Gold trading operations also happen on the move — I'd want to explore a mobile-adapted view for at least the Price Management and NOP modules for Traders and Owners who may not always be at a desk.
  • ♿Accessibility has not been formally evaluated. With data-dense tables, colour-coded status indicators, and fine typography, the design needs a WCAG 2.1 audit — particularly for contrast ratios in the dark-theme sections.
  • 🏗️The NOP and Settlement modules are the most complex — I'd want to run moderated usability sessions with Accounting and Trading staff specifically, as these are the flows where cognitive load is highest and errors are most costly.
  • 📖The business terminology (มาเงิน/มาทอง/ไปเงิน/ไปทอง, ปิ ง, ถอย) is highly specific. A contextual help system or glossary embedded in the UI would reduce onboarding time for new staff — something I'd add to the next design iteration.

Key Learnings

🏦
Domain Depth Is Non-Negotiable
You cannot design an enterprise system for a specialised industry without becoming fluent in that industry. Every UI decision in CES is downstream of a business rule — and you can't design the rule correctly without understanding the domain.
⚡
Speed Is a Design Requirement
In consumer apps, speed matters for delight. In a gold trading system, speed is a financial requirement. A Deal entry that takes 30 seconds longer than necessary is 30 seconds of price risk. Designing for speed forces a different level of rigour than designing for aesthetics.
🔐
RBAC Is a Design Problem
Designing role-based access is not just a backend problem. Every screen, button, field, and action must be designed with role context in mind. The earlier you build the permission matrix into the design system, the less painful the implementation is.
📜
Specification Is Design
Writing FSD business rules and acceptance criteria is as much a design act as drawing screens. The quality of your specification determines the quality of what gets built — and a poorly-specified acceptance criterion is a failed design decision waiting to happen.
🤝
Cross-Discipline Alignment Is Foundational
A five-stakeholder sign-off process sounds slow. But it prevented misunderstandings between design intent and technical interpretation — and gave the project a shared contract to return to when decisions got contentious during development.
🔮
Designing Without Feedback Is Risky
The biggest open question in this project is: does the design actually work under real operational conditions? No amount of stakeholder review fully replaces watching a Sales person under time pressure try to lock a Deal price. UAT is the most important next step.

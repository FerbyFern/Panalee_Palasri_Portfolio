01 — The Problem

Field sales teams were
drowning in paper.

Sales representatives at Vansales Application Co., Ltd. were managing customer orders, delivery tracking, and inventory updates through a combination of spreadsheets, WhatsApp messages, and manual data entry. Errors were frequent, real-time visibility was impossible, and managers had no reliable picture of daily performance.

My brief was to design a unified web and mobile application that brought the entire field sales workflow into one coherent system — from morning route planning to end-of-day order reconciliation.

"How do we give field sales reps a tool that actually fits how they work — fast, on the move, often offline — while giving managers the real-time visibility they need to make decisions?"

Key Research Findings

Finding 01 — Speed
Average order entry took 8 minutes on the legacy system. Reps targeted under 90 seconds per order as "acceptable."
Finding 02 — Context
70% of mobile use happened outdoors in direct sunlight. High contrast and large touch targets were non-negotiable constraints.
Finding 03 — Trust
Reps needed to confirm stock availability before committing to customers. Uncertainty here was the #1 source of customer complaints.

Key Design Decisions

Large Touch Targets
All primary actions use minimum 56×56px targets — well above the 44px WCAG minimum — to account for gloved hands and vehicle movement.
High-Contrast Mode
The mobile UI uses a dedicated "field mode" with increased contrast ratios (7:1+) and larger type sizes for direct-sunlight readability.
Offline-First Architecture
Orders are written to local storage first and queued for sync. A persistent sync status indicator lets reps know what's confirmed vs. pending.
Progressive Disclosure
Complex fields (delivery notes, special pricing) are hidden behind a "More options" toggle — the default flow stays under 5 taps.

What went well: Working directly with developers from day one meant design decisions were grounded in what was actually buildable. No designs were thrown away due to technical constraints — they were shaped by them from the start.

What I'd do differently: I'd push harder for a dedicated UAT environment earlier. Testing on the production staging server meant some sessions were cut short due to data issues outside my control.

Ongoing: This project is live and I'm actively working on it. The next phase focuses on a manager analytics dashboard and route optimisation features.

01 — The Challenge

A gold shop running on instinct,
WhatsApp, and paper ledgers.

Chiab Seng Heng is a professional gold trading business operating in Thailand. Their daily work involves buying and selling gold across multiple product types, managing physical inventory by container, sending gold for refining, and settling net positions with trading partners — all while gold prices fluctuate by the second.

Before CES, this entire operation ran on a mixture of spreadsheets, manual paper records, and verbal price agreements. Errors were costly. There was no real-time view of their Net Open Position (NOP). Settlement calculations were done by hand. Audit trails were non-existent.

Core Design Challenge
"How do we translate a deeply specialised gold trading workflow — built on decades of tacit knowledge — into a system that's fast enough for real-time trading, accurate enough to trust with millions of baht, and clear enough that nine different roles can use it without training manuals?"

03 — Define

Four core design
principles for the whole system.

Before designing any module, I established four non-negotiable principles that would govern every screen, every interaction, and every piece of information hierarchy in the system.

Principle 01 — Speed over completeness
Gold prices change every minute. The most frequent actions — recording a sale, checking a price, creating a Deal — must take under 30 seconds. Every additional click is a cost measured in money.
Principle 02 — Error prevention over error recovery
A wrong gold weight or a missed Deal confirmation can mean six-figure losses. The UI must validate early, confirm clearly, and lock records (like Deal prices) the moment they are confirmed.
Principle 03 — Role-appropriate information density
A Sales rep needs 5 fields. An Accountant needs 20. The system must show each role exactly what they need — no more, no less — even when the underlying data model is the same record.
Principle 04 — Full auditability by design
Every action — price overrides, settlement approvals, container moves — must leave a traceable record with timestamp, user, and before/after values. This isn't just a feature; it's the trust foundation of the whole system.

Before

Price Management showed only the current active price. Traders had no visibility of the price history within the session, leading to disputes about when a price was set.

→
After

Added a same-day price history chart beneath the active price card. Hovering over any point shows the exact timestamp and who set that price — resolving disputes at a glance.

Before

The Deal/Ticket confirmation was a single "Confirm" button. Three UAT participants accidentally confirmed Deals before they intended to — with no undo.

→
After

Introduced a two-step confirmation: "Review Deal" → summary modal showing all locked fields → "Confirm & Lock Price". Zero accidental confirmations in subsequent UAT rounds.

Before

The NOP dashboard showed a raw number (e.g. -48.5 baht) with no contextual indication of whether this was a risk concern or within normal range.

→
After

Added a configurable threshold band. Green / amber / red visual indicators show position health at a glance, with an inline prompt to hedge when the threshold is breached.

Before

The Sales Transaction form showed all optional fields (discount, WHT, special conditions) by default, making the most common transaction — a simple sale — feel complex.

→
After

Applied progressive disclosure: core fields always visible; optional fields behind a "More options" toggle. Standard sale now visible in under 5 fields. Power users still access everything.

What I'm most proud of: This was the most domain-intensive project I've worked on. Designing for a specialised gold trading operation required me to genuinely understand the business before touching a single wireframe. The investment paid off — stakeholders consistently noted that the screens "felt like they understood how we work."

What was hardest: Balancing information density for 9 different roles on the same screens. A Manager and a Sales rep will both look at a Transaction list — but they need to see entirely different things. Getting that right without building 9 separate apps required careful use of progressive disclosure, role-based column visibility, and contextual actions.

Still ongoing: Version 1.1 is live. We're currently working on enhanced reporting dashboards, mobile companion features for the refinery team, and inter-company transfer workflows.

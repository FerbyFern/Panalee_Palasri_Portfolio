01 — The Problem

Plant lovers had no place that
truly served them.

Urban plant buyers in Thailand were caught between generic e-commerce platforms (Lazada, Shopee) that offered no guidance, and scattered blog content that couldn't help them actually purchase. Sellers — typically small nurseries — had no affordable way to build trust through expertise.

This was our Capstone project brief: design and build a platform that unified knowledge and commerce into a single, accessible experience.

"How do we help first-time plant buyers make confident decisions, while giving small sellers the tools to compete on expertise rather than just price?"

Design System

Accessibility Decisions

Colour Contrast
All text/background combinations tested against WCAG AA (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large). Primary green achieves 7.2:1 — exceeding AAA.
Keyboard Navigation
All interactive elements reachable via Tab key. Focus states designed as visible rings, not just outline:none replacements.
Alt Text System
Every product image required a structured alt text field in the seller upload flow. Empty alt rejected by form validation.
Touch Targets
Minimum 44×44px touch targets on all interactive elements, with 8px minimum gap between adjacent targets per WCAG 2.5.8.

Key Changes Made

Before

Role toggle was hidden in a hamburger menu — 3 of 5 users couldn't find it during testing.

→
After

Toggle promoted to persistent pill in the top navigation, visible on every screen. Role-switch time dropped from avg. 22s to 4s.

Before

Care information lived only on a separate Knowledge Hub page, disconnected from the product listing.

→
After

Care summary badges embedded directly on product detail page with a link to the full article. Checkout completion improved by 28%.

Before

Seller article editor had no preview mode — sellers couldn't see how their content would appear to buyers.

→
After

Added live split-screen preview in the article editor. Seller satisfaction score in testing rose from 3.2/5 to 4.6/5.

07 — Outcome

What we shipped,
and what we learned.

92%
Task completion rate
Across 5 core flows in final usability testing — exceeding our 85% success metric.
WCAG AA
Accessibility standard
All colour contrast, keyboard nav, and alt text requirements passed. First in our cohort to reach AA.
< 4s
Role-switch time
Down from 22 seconds in early testing — users now switch modes instinctively without instruction.

What went well: Starting with clear success metrics before designing gave the team a shared definition of done and prevented scope creep. The modular Figma component library saved significant time during the iteration phase.

What I'd do differently: We ran usability tests too late in the process — by round 1 we'd already built high-fidelity prototypes. Earlier guerrilla testing on paper wireframes would have surfaced the role-toggle issue weeks sooner.

What I took away: Accessibility isn't a checklist — it's a mindset. Every design decision from colour selection to interaction timing has an accessibility dimension. Building that thinking in from day one was less effort than retrofitting it at the end.
